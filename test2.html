These notes provide an introduction to conditional expectations in von Neumann algebras and present the main ideas behind the proof of Takesaki's theorem. The material is aimed at graduate students with background in functional analysis.

---

1. Von Neumann Algebras

A von Neumann algebra \(\mathcal{M}\) is a *-subalgebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that is closed in the weak operator topology.

---

2. Conditional Expectations

A conditional expectation is a map \[ E: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N} \] satisfying:

\[ E(N_1 X N_2) = N_1 E(X) N_2 \]

for all \(N_1, N_2 \in \mathcal{N}\).

---

3. Modular Theory

Given a faithful normal state \(\phi\), one defines the modular operator \(\Delta\), which generates the modular automorphism group:

\[ \sigma_t(X) = \Delta^{it} X \Delta^{-it} \]
---

4. Takesaki's Theorem

Theorem. Let \(\mathcal{M}\) be a von Neumann algebra with faithful normal state \(\phi\), and let \(\mathcal{N} \subset \mathcal{M}\) be a von Neumann subalgebra invariant under the modular automorphism group. Then there exists a unique conditional expectation \(E: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{N}\) preserving \(\phi\).
---

5. Idea of the Proof

The proof relies on modular theory and the invariance of \(\mathcal{N}\) under the modular group. One constructs the expectation using the modular operator and shows it satisfies the required properties.
